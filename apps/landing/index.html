The problem

To know you are a student, a site makes you hand over your address.

Then it keeps it, mails it, and leaks it when it is breached. And it still cannot stop one person taking the free tier fifty times — ana+1@gmail.com and a.n.a@gmail.com are one inbox and two rows.

How it works

Three steps, and you never type your address.

  1. 01

    The site shows you a code

    It expires in minutes and authenticates for that one site, so it cannot be replayed anywhere else.

    MP-4KQ7-9XW2-3TFA
  2. 02

    You send yourself an email

    From the address you want to prove. Your provider signs it on the way out — and it will not sign a message claiming to come from a mailbox you do not control. That signature is the proof.

    From ana@udesa.edu.ar To ana@udesa.edu.ar MP-4KQ7-9XW2-3TFA DKIM · udesa.edu.ar
  3. 03

    The claim is spent, once

    Your mailbox becomes an unguessable value on Midnight. The site reads the domain and a first-time flag. The chain holds neither.

The SDK

Two calls, on your own server.

app/api/verify/route.ts 
// 1 · mint a code, show it to the user
const { code } = mailproof.startVerification()

// 2 · they hand back the message they sent themselves
const result = await mailproof.verify(eml)

if (result.ok && result.tier === 'STUDENT' && !result.alreadyClaimed) {
  await db.user.update({ where: { id }, data: { plan: 'PRO' } })
}

What comes back

tier
'STUDENT' — from your rules, or null
handle
one value per person, per campaign. Not reversible
alreadyClaimed
a field, not an error. It is the question you asked
trust
who read the message, stated in the result

Never returned: the address, the name, the inbox.

Straight answers

The six questions everybody asks.

If you never see my address, how do you email me?

We do not. The site you signed up to still has whatever address you gave it for notifications — that is a separate thing, and you chose it. MailProof only proves the institutional mailbox, and that one stays out of the database.

Why send myself an email instead of clicking a link?

Because a message someone sent to you proves nothing: whoever sent it chose the recipient. A message you sent is signed by your own provider, and it will not sign an address you do not control.

Can I just use a second mailbox and claim twice?

At a domain anyone can join, yes — which is why a generic address earns nothing here. The guarantee is exactly as strong as the difficulty of getting a second mailbox at that domain. A second university account is a real obstacle.

Who actually reads the email?

The attestor, which checks the message's own RSA signature. Not the site, and not the chain — but not nobody either. Every result says so in a trust field, and you can run the attestor yourself.

Why does this need a chain at all?

The "already claimed" list has to be public, owned by nobody, shareable between sites, and impossible to read backwards. A database gives you the last one only. A normal public chain loses it.

What happens when I graduate?

Campaigns run per period, so a benefit is re-proven rather than granted forever. Your old address may outlive your enrolment; the benefit does not.
